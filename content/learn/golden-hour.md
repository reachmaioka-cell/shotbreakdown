# How to get the golden hour look

Golden hour is a sun angle, not a LUT. The sun is low, the light is warm and directional, and the sky is still bright enough to fill shadows.

## When it actually happens

Roughly the last hour before sunset and the first after sunrise. Latitude and season change the clock. Watch the sun, not the time app.

## Placement

Put the sun behind or 45° off the subject if you want rim and glow. Put it camera-left or right if you want a hard key on the face. Do not stand them in open shade and grade warmth in later — that is a different shot.

## Camera

- Expose for skin, not the sky. Let the sky go hot.
- 35–50mm FF equivalent keeps the sun and the person in the same world.
- White balance around 5000–5600K and let the scene be warm. Do not crank Kelvin to 8000 unless you want cartoon orange.

## Ratio

Key (sun) to fill (sky bounce) often lands around 4:1 to 8:1. A white foam-core on the shadow side is enough. A silver bounce turns it into a music video.

## Common mistakes

Shooting 20 minutes after sunset and calling it golden hour. Adding a warm LED that fights the real sun. Heavy teal-orange LUTs that kill the actual color of that night.
