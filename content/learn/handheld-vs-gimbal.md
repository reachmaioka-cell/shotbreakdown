# Handheld vs gimbal

Handheld says a person is holding the camera. Gimbal says a machine is. Pick the grammar that matches the scene.

## Handheld

Elbows in, camera mass against your sternum, knees as suspension. Micro-drift and breath are the look. Good for documentary, panic, intimacy. Bad for horizon-critical wides and any shot that must match a locked master.

## Gimbal

Slow push-ins, walk-and-talks, and night exteriors where the frame must stay level. The sin is over-smoothing a scene that should feel human. If you cannot tell it is a gimbal, you probably wanted sticks.

## A simple test

Watch the edges of the frame. If verticals sway with the operator, it is handheld. If the world is on rails and the subject floats, it is a gimbal (or a Steadicam).

## Budget

A cage and a strap get you honest handheld. A used RS3 Mini gets you the night push-in. Do not rent a Ronin-2 for a talking-head interview.
